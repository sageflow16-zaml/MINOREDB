# INFORMATION SYSTEM PRINCIPLES
## Project Minore

**Status:** PROPOSED

---

## 1. Epistemic Separation

- **Principle Name**: Epistemic Separation
- **Statement**: Distinct epistemic states—source assertions, researcher interpretations, empirical evidence, and trading model rules—must remain distinguishable.
- **Rationale**: To prevent the conflation of source authority with empirical validity or researcher belief.
- **Authority Source**: `RECOVERED_PROJECT_MINORE_FOUNDING_DEFINITION_v0.1.md`
- **Implications**: Information entities representing different epistemic states must not be treated as equivalent.

---

## 2. Provenance Integrity

- **Principle Name**: Provenance Integrity
- **Statement**: All extracted knowledge must be associated with its originating source material.
- **Rationale**: To ensure the legitimacy and traceability of all research knowledge.
- **Authority Source**: `RECOVERED_PROJECT_MINORE_FOUNDING_DEFINITION_v0.1.md`
- **Implications**: Knowledge cannot be untethered from its origins.

---

## 3. Interpretive Distinction

- **Principle Name**: Interpretive Distinction
- **Statement**: Researcher interpretations must be held conceptually distinct from the source assertions they reference.
- **Rationale**: To maintain the distinction between what a source asserted and what a researcher believes.
- **Authority Source**: `RECOVERED_PROJECT_MINORE_FOUNDING_DEFINITION_v0.1.md`
- **Implications**: Assertions and interpretations are non-interchangeable.

---

## 4. Lineage Traceability

- **Principle Name**: Lineage Traceability
- **Statement**: A continuous, auditable chain of reasoning must exist from any derived knowledge back to its originating source claims.
- **Rationale**: To ensure the legitimacy and auditability of all research knowledge.
- **Authority Source**: `RECOVERED_PROJECT_MINORE_FOUNDING_DEFINITION_v0.1.md`
- **Implications**: Conclusions must be supportable by a documented path of derivation.
