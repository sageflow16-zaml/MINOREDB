## 1. Exact Semantic Distinctions

| **Provisional interpretation** | An interpretation that explicitly acknowledges uncertainty, unresolved contradiction, or pending empirical evidence. |
| **Authoritative interpretation** | The researcher's current, formally adopted position on what a concept means or encompasses. It is the reference interpretation for downstream research work until the researcher explicitly revises it. |

## 2. Minimum Options

### Option A — Source Consensus Required
The researcher may construct an interpretation only when all associated source claims agree or can be fully reconciled.

**Problem:** Directly contradicts D1 Rule 5 and A5 (which explicitly permit unresolved contradictions). Blocks interpretation construction whenever disagreement exists. Fails to answer: how does the researcher proceed with concept work when sources conflict?

### Option B — Researcher Selection without Constraint
The researcher may construct any interpretation without requirement to account for or acknowledge the available claim landscape.

**Problem:** Violates D1's requirement to preserve reasoning chain (D1 Capability 13). Interpretation becomes arbitrary; provenance is lost. Researcher interpretation would be indistinguishable from source claims.

### Option C — Constrained Researcher Authority with Reasoning Requirement
The researcher constructs an interpretation from the available claim landscape while required to:
- Account for all materially relevant claims in the available landscape (avoid silent dismissal of evidence)
- Explicitly reason from the claim landscape
- Preserve the full reasoning chain
- Acknowledge unresolved contradictions or tensions when present
- Permit provisional interpretations that acknowledge uncertainty

**Test against D1:** Preserves researcher authority (D1 Rule 2), permits evolution (D1 Rule 3), requires provenance chain (D1 Capability 13), permits unresolved contradictions (D1 Rule 5).

**Test against A5:** Respects contradiction determination; does not force false reconciliation.

**Verdict:** Option C is the minimum viable and founding-consistent standard.

## 3. Interpretation Construction Procedure

The following procedure governs how the researcher constructs an authoritative interpretation of a concept from the available claim landscape.

**Step 1 — Gather the available claim landscape**
Identify all extracted source claims currently associated with the concept per A4. Include all identified differences, variations, tensions, and genuine contradictions per A5.

**Step 2 — Assess the claim landscape**
Determine:
- Are all claims in broad agreement? (High consensus case)
- Are there compatible variations (different scope, context, emphasis)?
- Are there unresolved tensions (difficulty of reconciliation without full resolution)?
- Are there genuine unresolved contradictions?
- Are some claims more relevant to core concept definition than others?

**Step 3 — Determine the interpretation foundation**
Select which claims will directly inform the interpretation. This selection must be explicit and reasoned.

Selection may exclude claims based on:
- Relevance to core concept vs. peripheral variations
- Scope or applicability conditions
- Competing claims where the researcher judges one to be more foundational to the concept (with explicit reasoning)

Selection may **not** exclude claims based on:
- Silent dismissal without acknowledgment
- Mere disagreement with researcher preference without substantive reasoning
- Source authority alone (per A5 Rule 8)

**Step 4 — Construct the interpretation**
Formulate the researcher's authoritative understanding of the concept. The interpretation may:

a) **Reflect source consensus** — if all material claims agree, state the interpretation as consensus-derived

b) **Synthesize compatible variations** — if claims are variations on a compatible theme, synthesize into a unified understanding that encompasses the variations

c) **Acknowledge unresolved tension** — if claims create tension without strict incompatibility, construct an interpretation that acknowledges the tension explicitly

d) **Bracket genuine contradiction** — if genuine unresolved contradictions exist, construct an interpretation that provisionally adopts a position while explicitly preserving the contradiction

e) **Remain provisional** — acknowledge that the interpretation is provisional pending resolution of contradiction, additional source material, or empirical evidence

The interpretation may take the following forms:
- Consensus: "All sources agree that [concept] means [X]"
- Synthesized variation: "Sources describe [concept] with different emphases on [variations]. The interpretation integrates these as [synthesis]"
- Acknowledged tension: "Sources present [concept] with some interpretive difficulty between [positions]. The interpretation provisionally adopts [position] while noting the tension"
- Bracketed contradiction: "Sources conflict on whether [concept] requires [property]. The interpretation provisionally adopts [position] and preserves the unresolved contradiction"
- Provisional: "The interpretation of [concept] is [X], provisionally pending [specific uncertainty/evidence]"

**Step 5 — Document the reasoning chain**
Preserve explicit connection between:
- The interpretation adopted
- The available claim landscape considered
- Materiality judgments about which claims were foundational
- Treatment of contradictions and tensions
- Any explicit acknowledgment of uncertainty or provisionality

**Step 6 — Establish interpretation as authoritative**
The researcher formally adopts this interpretation as their current understanding of the concept. This interpretation is the authoritative reference interpretation for downstream research work unless explicitly revised.

## 4. Binding Methodology Rules

1. **Researcher interpretation is the researcher's authoritative current position on what a concept means or encompasses.** It is distinct from source claims (immutable, attributable) and from empirical validation (tested later). It represents the researcher's reasoned judgment.

2. **Interpretation must account for the available claim landscape.** The researcher must consider all materially relevant source claims in the available claim landscape. Silent dismissal of available materially relevant claims is prohibited.

3. **Interpretation foundation must be explicit.** The researcher must state which claims form the foundation of the interpretation and why. If claims are excluded from the foundation, the exclusion must be reasoned.

4. **Researcher authority permits interpretation beyond source consensus.** The researcher may construct interpretations that:
   - Synthesize multiple source claims into unified understanding not explicitly stated by any single source
   - Adopt a position in response to tension or contradiction, making an authoritative choice
   - Acknowledge provisionality and unresolved uncertainty

5. **Unresolved contradictions must not be silently reconciled.** If A5 identifies a genuine unresolved contradiction, the interpretation may provisionally adopt a position, but the contradiction must be explicitly preserved in the interpretation reasoning chain.

6. **Interpretations may be provisional.** An interpretation may explicitly acknowledge:
   - Unresolved contradiction in the source landscape
   - Pending empirical validation
   - Uncertainty pending additional source material
   - Tension requiring future resolution

A provisional interpretation is still the authoritative reference interpretation for downstream work unless explicitly revised.

7. **Interpretation does not alter source claims.** Per D1, source claims remain immutable and attributable. Interpretation is constructed from claims, not by rewriting them.

8. **Interpretation reasoning must preserve the full chain per D1 Capability 13.** The reasoning connecting interpretation to the available claim landscape must be explicit and traceable.

9. **Interpretation is not empirical validation.** The researcher's interpretation of what a concept means is distinct from evidence that the concept is true or effective in markets. Later empirical testing (M2, M3) may support or contradict the interpretation without invalidating the interpretation itself.

10. **Source authority does not override reasoning.** Per A5 Rule 8, a claim from a highly reputable source does not automatically become the interpretation foundation. The researcher's reasoning must justify the choice.

11. **Interpretation synthesis must preserve the semantic roles of claims as classified per A3.** Mechanistic and Predictive claims may inform concept understanding without being transformed into Definitional claims. When synthesizing claims of different types, the interpretation must make clear which aspects are definitional, which are mechanistic, and which are predictive.

12. **The current interpretation is the authoritative reference interpretation for downstream research work.** Downstream research references this interpretation unless explicitly departing with documented reasoning. Downstream research may question, test, contradict, or depart from the current interpretation, but may not silently treat another position as authoritative.
