DECISION A6 — RESEARCHER INTERPRETATION CONSTRUCTION (PROPOSED, under audit)
Point 1 Finding — "Complete Claim Landscape"
Exact finding:

A6 Step 1 requires: "Identify all extracted source claims associated with the concept per A4."

A6 Rule 2 requires: "The researcher must consider all materially relevant source claims."

The word "all" appears to create an absolute completeness standard.

Upstream authority test:

No ratified decision establishes that the researcher must have perfect knowledge of all possible claims that could exist anywhere. A1 admits research inputs as they are discovered; A2 extracts claims from admitted inputs; A4 associates them with concepts.

The claim landscape is necessarily bounded by what has been admitted, extracted, and associated at the time of interpretation construction.

Necessity test:

Does A6 require absolute omniscience, or only accountability for the currently available processed claims?

The protective requirement is: the researcher must not silently dismiss available material evidence. The requirement is not that undiscovered material must be discovered before interpretation is permitted.

Whether a real inconsistency exists:

Yes. "Complete claim landscape" and "all materially relevant claims" create an impossible epistemic standard if interpreted as requiring knowledge of undiscovered sources.

Minimum correction required:

Replace "complete claim landscape" with "available claim landscape" throughout.

Clarify Step 1: "Identify all extracted source claims currently associated with the concept per A4."

Clarify Rule 2: "The researcher must consider all materially relevant source claims in the available claim landscape. Silent dismissal of available claims is prohibited."

Point 2 Finding — Reliability Weighting in Step 3
Exact finding:

A6 Step 3 permits foundation-selection exclusion based on: "Explicit judgment that a claim is less reliable" and states: "the researcher may weigh evidence of reliability."

Upstream authority test:

A1 establishes provenance uncertainty but does not disqualify claims (Rule 3)
A5 establishes that authority does not resolve contradiction (Rule 8)
No ratified decision establishes a reliability-weighting framework
Semantic distinction test:

What does "reliability" mean in A6's usage?

Provenance confidence (A1 territory — already handled)
Source authority (A5 explicitly rejects this as a resolution mechanism)
Truth of the claim (empirical validation — not yet reached)
Interpretive judgment about which claims are more foundational (legitimate researcher authority)
The term "reliability" conflates these dimensions without definition.

Necessity test:

Does A6 need reliability weighting, or only explicit reasoning for interpretive choices?

The protective requirement is: the researcher must explicitly reason about why certain claims inform the interpretation and others do not. The requirement is not to introduce a reliability metric.

A6 already permits exclusion based on: "relevance to core concept vs. peripheral variations" and "scope or applicability conditions." These are sufficient without introducing "reliability."

Whether a real inconsistency exists:

Yes. "Reliability" introduces an undefined weighting dimension that may silently import source authority, truth claims, or provenance confidence without proper semantic grounding.

Minimum correction required:

Remove "explicit judgment that a claim is less reliable" from the permitted exclusion list.

Remove "the researcher may weigh evidence of reliability."

Retain only: relevance to core concept, scope/applicability conditions, and competing claims that appear more foundational (with explicit reasoning required for all exclusions).

Point 3 Finding — "Binding on Downstream Research Work"

A6 defines authoritative interpretation as: "binding on downstream research decisions until the researcher explicitly revises it" (Step 6) and "binding on downstream research work" (Rule 6).

Upstream authority test:

Semantic distinction test:

What does "binding" legitimately mean?

The current reference point for downstream work (valid — downstream research must know what the current interpretation is)

Necessity test:

Does "binding" serve A6's purpose, or does it overreach into constraining future research?

The protective requirement is: downstream research must reference the current interpretation and make explicit when departing from it. The requirement is not that the interpretation is unquestionable.

Yes. "Binding" is semantically ambiguous and may be read as preventing downstream research from questioning the interpretation, which would contradict the research process itself (empirical testing may contradict interpretations).

Minimum correction required:

Replace "binding on downstream research work" with: "the authoritative reference interpretation for downstream research work. Downstream research references this interpretation unless explicitly departing with documented reasoning."

Remove the word "binding" from Step 6 and Rule 6.

Point 4 Finding — Test 1 and Semantic Laundering
Exact finding:

Test 1 combines:

Claim A: Definitional (structural definition)
Claim B: Mechanistic (mechanism description)
Claim C: Predictive (outcome/behavior)
into a unified interpretation that synthesizes all three into what a Fair Value Gap is.

Upstream authority test:

A3 establishes that classification determines what kind of assertion the source made. Classification is preserved and must not be altered (A3 Rule 2).

A6 Rule 7 states: "Interpretation does not alter source claims."

Semantic distinction test:

Does combining a Mechanistic claim and a Predictive claim into a Definitional interpretation alter the semantic role of those claims?

Claim B states how FVGs work (mechanism). Claim C states what happens with FVGs (prediction). Test 1's interpretation treats both as part of what FVGs are (definition).

This is semantic role transformation: a mechanistic explanation is being incorporated into a definitional understanding. The claim itself is not altered, but its semantic role in the interpretation is shifted.

Necessity test:

May a researcher's interpretation synthesize claims with different A3 classifications into a unified understanding?

Yes — this is legitimate researcher synthesis. The error is not the synthesis itself but the failure to preserve the semantic distinction between:

What FVGs structurally are (Definitional claims)
How they work (Mechanistic claims)
What they predict (Predictive claims)
The interpretation should make clear which aspects are definitional, which are mechanistic, and which are predictive, even when synthesizing them.

Whether a real inconsistency exists:

Yes. Test 1 silently launders Mechanistic and Predictive claims into a Definitional synthesis without preserving their distinct semantic roles.

Minimum correction required:

Revise Test 1 interpretation to:

"A Fair Value Gap is defined structurally as a three-candle imbalance where candles 1 and 3 wicks do not overlap (Definitional, Claim A). This structure represents a price inefficiency mechanism (Mechanistic, Claim B) that typically results in gap-fill behavior (Predictive, Claim C). The interpretation integrates these three complementary aspects while preserving their distinct semantic roles."

Add a binding rule: "Interpretation synthesis must preserve the semantic roles of claims as classified per A3. Mechanistic and Predictive claims may inform concept understanding without being transformed into Definitional claims."
