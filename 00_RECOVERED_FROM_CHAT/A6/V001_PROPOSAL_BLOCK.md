## 1. Exact Semantic Distinctions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Source claim** | An extracted, classified, and concept-associated claim attributed to a research input source (A1–A4). Immutable and attributable per D1. |
| **Claim landscape** | The complete set of source claims associated with a concept, including all differences, variations, tensions, and genuine contradictions identified per A5. |
| **Researcher interpretation** | The researcher's current authoritative understanding of a concept, constructed from the claim landscape. It represents what the researcher currently believes about the concept. |
| **Interpretation foundation** | The subset of source claims and reasoning from the claim landscape that directly inform the researcher's interpretation. |
| **Interpretation reasoning** | The explicit logical or evidential reasoning by which the researcher arrived at their interpretation from the claim landscape. |
| **Source-derived interpretation** | An interpretation that directly reflects or synthesizes what sources explicitly stated. |
| **Researcher synthesis** | An interpretation that synthesizes, integrates, or generalizes from multiple source claims in ways that go beyond any single source's explicit assertion. |
| **Provisional interpretation** | An interpretation that explicitly acknowledges uncertainty, unresolved contradiction, or pending empirical evidence. |
| **Authoritative interpretation** | The researcher's current, formally adopted position on what a concept means or encompasses. It is binding on downstream research decisions until the researcher explicitly revises it. |
| **Interpretation evolution** | A change in interpretation from one version to another. The prior version is preserved per D1 Rule 4. |

## 2. Minimum Options

### Option A — Source Consensus Required
The researcher may construct an interpretation only when all associated source claims agree or can be fully reconciled.

**Problem:** Directly contradicts D1 Rule 5 and A5 (which explicitly permit unresolved contradictions). Blocks interpretation construction whenever disagreement exists. Fails to answer: how does the researcher proceed with concept work when sources conflict?

### Option B — Researcher Selection without Constraint
The researcher may construct any interpretation without requirement to account for or acknowledge the claim landscape.

**Problem:** Violates D1's requirement to preserve reasoning chain (D1 Capability 13). Interpretation becomes arbitrary; provenance is lost. Researcher interpretation would be indistinguishable from source claims.

### Option C — Constrained Researcher Authority with Reasoning Requirement
The researcher constructs an interpretation from the claim landscape while required to:
- Account for all materially relevant claims (avoid silent dismissal of evidence)
- Explicitly reason from the claim landscape
- Preserve the full reasoning chain
- Acknowledge unresolved contradictions or tensions when present
- Permit provisional interpretations that acknowledge uncertainty

**Test against D1:** Preserves researcher authority (D1 Rule 2), permits evolution (D1 Rule 3), requires provenance chain (D1 Capability 13), permits unresolved contradictions (D1 Rule 5).

**Test against A5:** Respects contradiction determination; does not force false reconciliation.

**Verdict:** Option C is the minimum viable and founding-consistent standard.

## 3. Interpretation Construction Procedure

The following procedure governs how the researcher constructs an authoritative interpretation of a concept from the associated claim landscape.

**Step 1 — Gather the complete claim landscape**
Identify all extracted source claims associated with the concept per A4. Include all identified differences, variations, tensions, and genuine contradictions per A5.

**Step 2 — Assess the claim landscape**
Determine:
- Are all claims in broad agreement? (High consensus case)
- Are there compatible variations (different scope, context, emphasis)?
- Are there unresolved tensions (difficulty of reconciliation without full resolution)?
- Are there genuine unresolved contradictions?
- Are some claims more relevant to core concept definition than others?

**Step 3 — Determine the interpretation foundation**
Select which claims will directly inform the interpretation. This selection must be explicit and reasoned.

Selection may exclude claims based on:
- Relevance to core concept vs. peripheral variations
- Scope or applicability conditions
- Explicit judgment that a claim is less reliable (per A1 Rule 3, uncertainty does not disqualify; however, the researcher may weigh evidence of reliability)
- Competing claims that appear more foundational

Selection may **not** exclude claims based on:
- Silent dismissal without acknowledgment
- Mere disagreement with researcher preference
- Source authority alone (per A5 Rule 8)

**Step 4 — Construct the interpretation**
Formulate the researcher's authoritative understanding of the concept. The interpretation may:

a) **Reflect source consensus** — if all material claims agree, state the interpretation as consensus-derived

b) **Synthesize compatible variations** — if claims are variations on a compatible theme, synthesize into a unified understanding that encompasses the variations

c) **Acknowledge unresolved tension** — if claims create tension without strict incompatibility, construct an interpretation that acknowledges the tension explicitly

d) **Bracket genuine contradiction** — if genuine unresolved contradictions exist, construct an interpretation that provisionally adopts a position while explicitly preserving the contradiction

e) **Remain provisional** — acknowledge that the interpretation is provisional pending resolution of contradiction, additional source material, or empirical evidence

**Step 5 — Document the reasoning chain**
Preserve explicit connection between:
- The interpretation adopted
- The claim landscape considered
- Materiality judgments about which claims were foundational
- Treatment of contradictions and tensions
- Any explicit acknowledgment of uncertainty or provisionality

**Step 6 — Establish interpretation as authoritative**
The researcher formally adopts this interpretation as their current understanding of the concept. This interpretation is binding on downstream research work (B1, M2, etc.) unless explicitly revised.

## 4. Binding Methodology Rules

1. **Researcher interpretation is the researcher's authoritative current position on what a concept means or encompasses.** It is distinct from source claims (immutable, attributable) and from empirical validation (tested later). It represents the researcher's reasoned judgment.

2. **Interpretation must account for the claim landscape.** The researcher must consider all materially relevant source claims associated with the concept. Silent dismissal of claims is prohibited.

3. **Interpretation foundation must be explicit.** The researcher must state which claims form the foundation of the interpretation and why. If claims are excluded from the foundation, the exclusion must be reasoned.

4. **Researcher authority permits interpretation beyond source consensus.** The researcher may construct interpretations that:
   - Synthesize multiple source claims into unified understanding not explicitly stated by any single source
   - Adopt a position in response to tension or contradiction, making an authoritative choice
   - Acknowledge provisionality and unresolved uncertainty

5. **Unresolved contradictions must not be silently reconciled.** If A5 identifies a genuine unresolved contradiction, the interpretation may provisionally adopt a position, but the contradiction must be explicitly preserved in the interpretation reasoning chain.

6. **Interpretations may be provisional.** An interpretation may explicitly acknowledge:
   - Unresolved contradiction in the source landscape
   - Pending empirical validation
   - Uncertainty pending additional source material
   - Tension requiring future resolution

A provisional interpretation is still authoritative and binding on downstream work unless explicitly revised.

7. **Interpretation does not alter source claims.** Per D1, source claims remain immutable and attributable. Interpretation is constructed from claims, not by rewriting them.

8. **Interpretation reasoning must preserve the full chain per D1 Capability 13.** The reasoning connecting interpretation to claim landscape must be explicit and traceable.

9. **Interpretation is not empirical validation.** The researcher's interpretation of what a concept means is distinct from evidence that the concept is true or effective in markets. Later empirical testing (M2, M3) may support or contradict the interpretation without invalidating the interpretation itself.

10. **Source authority does not override reasoning.** Per A5 Rule 8, a claim from a highly reputable source does not automatically become the interpretation foundation. The researcher's reasoning must justify the choice.

11. **Interpretation evolution is preserved.** When interpretation changes, the prior version is preserved as historical provenance per D1 Rule 4. The reasoning for the change is documented.

## 5. Interpretation Construction Procedure — Detailed Application

**Step 1 input:** The complete claim landscape for the concept

**Step 2 assessment:** Classify the landscape into categories (consensus, variations, tensions, contradictions)

**Step 3 foundation selection:**
- Identify core defining claims
- Identify peripheral or conditional claims
- Mark claims excluded from foundation (with reasoning)
- Mark tensions and contradictions that will be addressed in Step 4

**Step 4 construction:** Formulate interpretation using one or more patterns:
- Consensus: "All sources agree that [concept] means [X]"
- Synthesized variation: "Sources describe [concept] with different emphases on [variations]. The interpretation integrates these as [synthesis]"
- Acknowledged tension: "Sources present [concept] with some interpretive difficulty between [positions]. The interpretation provisionally adopts [position] while noting the tension"
- Bracketed contradiction: "Sources conflict on whether [concept] requires [property]. The interpretation provisionally adopts [position] and preserves the unresolved contradiction"
- Provisional: "The interpretation of [concept] is [X], provisionally pending [specific uncertainty/evidence]"

**Step 5 output:** Documented reasoning chain connecting interpretation to landscape

**Step 6 authorization:** Formal adoption as current authoritative understanding

## 6. Adversarial Tests

**Test 1 — Broad agreement across sources**

Concept: Fair Value Gap
Source Claims:
- Claim A (Source 1): "A Fair Value Gap is a three-candle imbalance where candles 1 and 3 wicks do not overlap"
- Claim B (Source 2): "Fair Value Gaps are price inefficiencies created when price moves without trading in a zone"
- Claim C (Source 3): "FVGs form when price gaps over support/resistance levels without filling the gap"

Claim Landscape Assessment:
- Claim A: Structural definition
- Claim B: Mechanism description
- Claim C: Outcome/behavior description
- All three are compatible; they describe different aspects of the same phenomenon

Interpretation Construction:
- Foundation: All three claims inform the core definition
- Synthesis: "A Fair Value Gap is a three-candle imbalance structure (Claim A) that represents price inefficiency (Claim B) and typically results in gap-fill behavior (Claim C). These aspects are complementary and mutually reinforcing."
- Provisional status: Not provisional; consensus across sources
- Framework handling: ✓ Synthesizes multiple compatible variations into unified interpretation

**Test 2 — Multiple compatible variations**

Concept: Order Block
Source Claims:
- Claim A (Source 1): "Order Blocks form from bearish consolidation before bullish impulses"
- Claim B (Source 2): "Order Blocks form from the last consolidation before any impulse, regardless of direction"
- Claim C (Source 3): "Order Blocks are consolidations that precede significant price movements; the specific impulse direction varies"

Claim Landscape Assessment:
- Claim A: Specific (bearish consolidation → bullish impulse)
- Claim B: General (consolidation → any impulse)
- Claim C: General with variations
- Tension: Claim A is more specific; Claims B and C are broader. Not contradictory, but varying scope

Interpretation Construction:
- Foundation: All three claims; Claim A is more specific, Claims B and C capture breadth
- Synthesis: "An Order Block is a consolidation that precedes an impulse. While impulses can be directional (Claim A's bearish-before-bullish pattern) or bidirectional (Claims B, C), the core definition is the consolidation-impulse relationship. Sources suggest the bearish-to-bullish pattern is common but not universal."
- Provisional status: Not provisional; the variation is resolvable as scope differences
- Framework handling: ✓ Interpretation encompasses variations by distinguishing core definition from scope variations

**Test 3 — Unresolved tension**

Concept: Optimal Trade Entry
Source Claims:
- Claim A (Source 1): "Optimal Trade Entry occurs at the 61.8% retracement of the impulse"
- Claim B (Source 2): "Optimal Trade Entry occurs at the 50% retracement"
- Claim C (Source 3): "Optimal Trade Entry varies based on impulse strength; typically 50–70% retracement"

Claim Landscape Assessment:
- Claim A and B: Conflicting specific numbers (61.8% vs. 50%)
- Claim C: Range acknowledgment
- Tension: Multiple sources assert different specific entry levels; Claim C acknowledges variation but does not fully resolve the conflict

Contradiction Analysis (A5): Is this a genuine contradiction?
- Claim A asserts 61.8% specifically
- Claim B asserts 50% specifically
- If applied to the same impulse under the same conditions, both cannot be optimal
- Genuine contradiction (unresolved per A5)

Interpretation Construction:
- Foundation: All three claims; acknowledge that sources conflict on the specific level
- Interpretation (provisional): "Optimal Trade Entry in this framework is provisionally interpreted as occurring in the 50–70% retracement range, with debate in source material between 50% (Claim B) and 61.8% (Claim A) as the ideal point. The tension between these specific levels remains unresolved."
- Provisional status: Yes — explicitly acknowledges the unresolved contradiction
- Framework handling: ✓ Interpretation acknowledges unresolved contradiction without forcing false reconciliation; remains authoritative despite provisionality

**Test 4 — Genuine unresolved contradiction**

Concept: Market Maker Intent
Source Claims:
- Claim A (Source 1): "Market Makers actively hunt stop-losses to accumulate liquidity for their trades"
- Claim B (Source 2): "Market Makers' stop-loss hunting is a side effect of their natural order flow, not intentional"

Claim Landscape Assessment:
- Direct logical incompatibility: intentional vs. unintentional behavior
- Applied to the same market conditions and same Market Makers
- No context resolves this contradiction (A5 Step 2)
- Genuine unresolved contradiction per A5

Interpretation Construction:
- Foundation: Both claims present; researcher acknowledges the contradiction
- Interpretation (bracketed contradiction): "The interpretation of Market Maker Intent is that Market Makers' behavior creates stop-loss hunting effects (both sources agree on the behavior). However, sources disagree on whether this is intentional (Claim A) or a side effect of natural order flow (Claim B). The interpretation provisionally treats it as intentional behavior while explicitly preserving the contradiction about intent."
- Provisional status: Yes — explicitly brackets and preserves the unresolved contradiction
- Researcher choice: The researcher has selected intentionality as the provisional foundation, but justified the choice by acknowledging the competing claim
- Framework handling: ✓ Interpretation does not force reconciliation; does not silently dismiss Claim B; makes an authoritative choice while preserving the contradiction

The distinction may depend on timeframe or market conditions not yet fully specified in source material. The interpretation provisionally adopts [specific position] based on [reasoning about which conditions dominate in the research domain], while acknowledging the competing perspective."
- Provisional status: Yes — acknowledges tension and dependence on unspecified conditions
- Framework handling: ✓ Researcher makes a choice but justifies it by reasoning about conditions, not by source authority alone; preserves competing claim; makes reasoned assertion
