DECISION A7 — INTERPRETATION EVOLUTION TRIGGERS (PROPOSED, under audit)
Finding 1 — "Available Research State" vs. A6's Bounded Claim Landscape
Exact tension:

A7 defines "available research state" as: "currently admitted and processed research inputs, extracted claims, preserved contexts, claim classifications, concept associations, and contradiction statuses."

This definition encompasses the full output of A1–A5 plus empirical results from later stages. It is broader than A6's "available claim landscape" (bounded by admitted/processed/associated claims).

A7 then uses "available research state" as the domain within which trigger materiality is assessed (Step 2, Rule 12).

Upstream authority test:

A6 establishes the bounded "available claim landscape" — what is currently admitted, extracted, and associated — as the working domain for interpretation construction. A6 does not introduce "available research state" as a term.

The Founding Definition uses "research state" implicitly (pipeline stages) but does not define it as a semantic unit.

A7 is generating a new composite term not grounded in any ratified definition.

Necessity test:

Does A7 need "available research state" specifically, or can it operate within each component's own established boundary?

A7 references changes across multiple stages (A1–A5 changes, empirical evidence). It could instead refer directly to changes in specific outputs: "changes in the available claim landscape," "changes in A5 status," "new empirical evidence," etc. A composite term is not necessary.

Whether a real inconsistency exists:

Yes. "Available research state" is an undefined composite term. It implicitly expands A6's bounded domain without ratified authority to do so.

Minimum correction required:

Remove "available research state" as a defined term and throughout A7. Replace all occurrences with specific references to their authoritative source: "available claim landscape" (A6), "A5 status," "preserved source context" (A2), "concept associations" (A4), "empirical evidence" (future stages). This eliminates an unratified composite term while preserving A7's substantive content.

Finding 2 — Evidence-Strength Assessment Before M3
Exact tension:

A7's Trigger Determination Framework distinguishes:

"Empirical evidence that materially contradicts or undermines claims" → mandatory trigger
"Weak or inconclusive empirical results" → permissive trigger
Test 5 identifies new empirical results as a mandatory trigger. Test 6 classifies "mixed results with wide uncertainty" as permissive.

A7's Rule 8 states: "A7 does not decide whether evidence is strong enough to force evolution."

Upstream authority test:

M3 — Experimental Rigor and Evidence Assessment is not yet ratified. No upstream ratified decision defines "weak," "inconclusive," "materially contradicts," or "mixed results."

A7 is drawing distinctions between evidence quality levels using undefined criteria — exactly what M3 will define.

Necessity test:

Can A7 define an evidence-based trigger without knowing what constitutes sufficient evidence?

A7 needs to say only: "New empirical evidence bearing on foundational claims is a reconsideration trigger." It does not need to distinguish mandatory vs. permissive based on evidence strength (which is M3 territory).

The mandatory/permissive distinction for empirical evidence specifically requires an evidence-quality judgment that A7 does not have authority to make.

Whether a real inconsistency exists:

Yes. The mandatory vs. permissive distinction for empirical evidence is implicitly an evidence-strength assessment, contradicting the explicit disclaimer in Rule 8 and overreaching into M3 territory.

Minimum correction required:

Collapse the empirical evidence trigger into a single rule: "New empirical evidence bearing materially on claims the current interpretation depends on is a reconsideration trigger. Whether that trigger is mandatory or permissive depends on evidence assessment criteria established in M3. A7 does not determine evidence strength."

Remove the mandatory/permissive distinction for empirical evidence from Step 3 and Rules 8. Remove Tests 5 and 6 or consolidate them into one test that acknowledges the M3 dependency.

Classification: CORRECTION REQUIRED

Finding 3 — Internal Coherence of Mandatory / Permissive / Insufficient Model
Exact tension:

A7 establishes three trigger categories:

Mandatory: requires reconsideration
Permissive: allows reconsideration
Insufficient: does not justify reconsideration
The semantic function of "mandatory" is: the researcher must reconsider (but may retain the interpretation after reconsideration with explicit reasoning).

The semantic function of "permissive" is: the researcher may reconsider (but is not required to).

The semantic function of "insufficient" is: no justification for reconsideration exists.

Test of internal coherence:

The categories are semantically distinct:

Mandatory preserves researcher authority (evolution not forced) while requiring engagement
Permissive respects researcher judgment without requiring engagement
Insufficient prevents arbitrary shifts (preference, silent substitution)
However, "Permissive" creates an ambiguity: if reconsideration is permitted but not required after a non-foundational relevant change, can the researcher permanently ignore materially relevant new claims on a non-foundational peripheral aspect?

A6 Rule 2 states: "silent dismissal of available materially relevant claims is prohibited."

If a new claim is materially relevant, A6 prohibits silent dismissal, which may mean reconsideration is always required for materially relevant changes — making "permissive" a category without a clear, coherent domain.

Upstream authority test:

A6 prohibits silent dismissal of materially relevant claims. If any new claim is materially relevant, it must be accounted for. A7's permissive category may apply only to claims that are relevant but not materially relevant (a distinction A7 does not currently draw clearly).

Whether a real inconsistency exists:

Partial. A7's trigger model is internally coherent for its intended purpose, but the "permissive" category risks blurring into "mandatory" for anything truly materially relevant (due to A6's prohibition). The boundary between mandatory and permissive is not clearly defined.

Minimum correction required:

Clarify that permissive triggers apply to claims/changes that are relevant but not materially relevant (i.e., do not bear on the interpretation foundation). Any change that is materially relevant triggers mandatory reconsideration per A6. Revise the Step 3 criteria to make this explicit.

Classification: NARROW CLARIFICATION

Finding 4 — "Materiality to Interpretation Foundation" as Grounded Judgment Standard
Exact tension:

A7's entire trigger model rests on "materiality to interpretation foundation." Step 2 asks: "Does the change affect claims, context, or contradiction statuses that the current interpretation explicitly relied on as its foundation?"

This is defined in terms of the A6 interpretation foundation (the explicit foundation documented in Step 3 of A6).

Upstream authority test:

A6 establishes that the interpretation foundation is explicit and reasoned (A6 Step 3, Rule 3). The interpretation foundation is a documented product of A6. Referring to it in A7 is therefore grounded.

Necessity test:

Is "materiality to interpretation foundation" defined clearly enough to be applied without hidden judgment?

Since A6 requires the foundation to be explicit, the question "does this change affect the foundation?" is answerable by reference to the A6 interpretation document itself. This is a well-grounded standard.

Whether a real inconsistency exists:

No. Materiality to interpretation foundation is sufficiently grounded in A6's explicit foundation requirement. No hidden judgment standard is introduced.

Minimum correction required:

None substantively required. A narrow clarification may be added to confirm that materiality is assessed against the A6-documented interpretation foundation record, not against a new judgment standard.

Classification: NO CORRECTION REQUIRED

Finding 5 — Mandatory Trigger Based on Unvalidated Change
Exact tension:

A7 Rule 6 states: "Changes in concept association can be mandatory triggers if they add/remove substantively relevant claims from the concept's available claim landscape in a way that materially affects the interpretation foundation."

A4 governs concept association. A4 Rule 8 states concept association does not alter claims; it is metadata about subject matter.

A7 Rule 5 states: "Changes in claim classification or classification uncertainty can be mandatory triggers."

A3 governs classification. A3 Rule 2: classification must not alter the extracted claim.

Test:

If an A4 reassociation is in error (e.g., a claim is incorrectly newly associated with a concept), A7 would trigger mandatory reconsideration based on an incorrect association. Similarly, if an A3 reclassification is incorrect, A7 would trigger reconsideration based on an incorrect classification.

However: A7 is not reviewing the validity of A4 or A3 outputs. It is responding to them.

Upstream authority test:

A7 must inherit A4 and A3 outputs without re-performing them. Changes to concept association and classification are A4/A3 outputs, not A7 decisions. A7 responding to them is correct.

Necessity test:

Should A7 require reconsideration based on any A4/A3 change, or only validated ones?

A7 has no authority to validate A4/A3 outputs. If an association or classification changes, A7 responds to that change. The validity of A4/A3 output is an A4/A3 concern. A7 responding to changes is correct and does not validate them.

Whether a real inconsistency exists:

No material inconsistency. A7 correctly inherits A3/A4 outputs. However, a narrow clarification would help: A7 responds to documented changes in A3/A4 status as delivered; it does not re-examine whether those changes were correctly made.

Minimum correction required:

Narrow clarification: Add a note in Rule 5 and Rule 6 that A7 responds to documented changes in classification or association as inherited from A3/A4 without re-performing those upstream determinations.

Classification: NARROW CLARIFICATION

Finding 6 — Distinction Between Reconsideration, Evolution, Downstream Departure, and Empirical Challenge
Exact tension:

A7's semantic distinctions table defines:

Reconsideration: re-evaluating whether current interpretation remains justified
Interpretation evolution: change from current to revised interpretation
Downstream departure: A6 Rule 12 reference
Empirical challenge: not explicitly defined as a distinct term
Within the binding rules, downstream departure (Rule 11) is treated as "not a revision trigger by itself" but "may become permissive if it reveals a substantive limitation."

Test:

Is "downstream departure becoming a permissive trigger when it reveals a substantive limitation" coherent?

A downstream departure is an explicit act by the researcher (A6 Rule 12). If the researcher explicitly departs and finds the departure reveals a problem, they can initiate reconsideration based on that finding. The trigger is actually the researcher's substantive finding, not the departure itself.

Whether a real inconsistency exists:

Minor. The formulation "downstream departure may become permissive trigger" is slightly circular — what actually triggers reconsideration is the substantive finding from the departure, not the departure per se. The current wording could lead to treating downstream departures themselves as triggers rather than what they reveal.

Minimum correction required:

Revise Rule 11 to clarify: downstream departure does not trigger reconsideration. If a downstream departure reveals a substantive incompatibility or limitation in the current interpretation, that finding may itself be a permissive trigger. The trigger is the finding, not the departure.

Classification: NARROW CLARIFICATION

Finding 7 — Revision Responsibility Assigned to Unresolved Procedure
Exact tension:

A7 repeatedly references "revision" being deferred: "A7 governs triggers for reconsideration/evolution, not the revision procedure itself." Section 8 lists "Procedure for performing an interpretation revision once reconsideration is triggered" as unresolved methodology content.

No ratified decision defines the revision procedure.

Test:

Is it coherent to ratify trigger criteria without a defined revision procedure?

Triggers can be defined independently of revision procedure in the same way that A5 identifies contradictions without defining how researchers reconcile them. The trigger is a precondition; the procedure follows it.

Upstream authority test:

A6 acknowledges that interpretations may evolve and A7 governs triggers. The revision procedure is acknowledged as unresolved. A6 Rule 12 and D1 Rules 3–4 together establish that evolution is permitted and provenance is preserved — sufficient to authorize trigger definition without fully defining the revision procedure.

Whether a real inconsistency exists:

No. Defining triggers without defining revision procedure is coherent and explicitly scoped. The remaining-questions section correctly defers revision procedure.

Minimum correction required:

None.

Classification: NO CORRECTION REQUIRED

Finding 8 — Hidden Completeness Requirements or Review Obligations
Exact tension:

A7 Rule 2: "If a change materially affects the interpretation foundation... the researcher must reconsider the interpretation."

Does "must reconsider" create a hidden periodic review obligation or completeness requirement over unknown material?

Test:

A7 Rule 12 states: "Triggers are evaluated against the bounded available claim landscape (A6). The existence of unknown or undiscovered material is not itself a trigger."

This correctly bounds the completeness problem.

However: Rule 2's "must reconsider" applies whenever a material change occurs. If multiple material changes accumulate without reconsideration, does A7 impose a mandatory backlog review?

A7 does not address what happens when multiple mandatory triggers accumulate without reconsideration being performed. This could create an implied lifecycle obligation.

Upstream authority test:

D1 does not define a reconsideration schedule. A6 requires accounting for the available claim landscape at interpretation construction time but does not impose periodic review. No ratified decision authorizes a mandatory review cycle.

Whether a real inconsistency exists:

Minor latent ambiguity. "Must reconsider" could be interpreted as creating an immediate obligation on every trigger event, effectively creating a review lifecycle. A7 should clarify that mandatory reconsideration means the researcher must address the trigger before treating the current interpretation as a settled reference for new downstream decisions that depend on the changed foundation — not that every trigger requires an immediately performed review session.

Minimum correction required:

Clarify Rule 2: mandatory reconsideration means the researcher must account for the trigger before relying on the current interpretation for downstream decisions that depend on the changed foundation. No periodic review schedule or backlog obligation is implied.

Classification: NARROW CLARIFICATION
