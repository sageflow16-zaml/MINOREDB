## 1. Exact Semantic Distinctions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Research question** | A substantively grounded question about the research domain that the researcher identifies as worth investigating. May arise from documented conditions in the current research state or from researcher-generated inquiry introducing new research directions within the domain. |
| **Source claim** | What a source said (A1–A2). Immutable and attributable (D1). Research questions may arise from claims but are not themselves claims. |
| **Researcher interpretation** | The current authoritative understanding of a concept (A6). Research questions may identify what the interpretation does not adequately address or may investigate the interpretation itself, but identifying a question does not modify the interpretation. |
| **Unresolved contradiction** | Claims that cannot both be true simultaneously (A5), preserved without forced reconciliation. May be an origin for research questions but does not automatically become one. |
| **Research gap** | An aspect of the research domain where the current interpretation or available claim landscape provides insufficient, uncertain, incomplete, or absent understanding. |
| **Arbitrary curiosity** | Speculation lacking articulated substantive domain-grounded reasoning or connection to research objectives. |
| **Research-state-responsive inquiry** | Questions arising from documented conditions in the current research state (contradictions, gaps, tensions, limitations, anomalies). |
| **Researcher-generated grounded inquiry** | Questions introducing new research directions within the domain, grounded in substantive domain reasoning and research objectives, even without prior documentation in the current research state. |
| **Hypothesis** | A testable proposition formulated in response to a research question (later stages; not B1). Research questions precede hypotheses. |
| **Research-question origin** | The condition that gives rise to identifying a research question — may be a documented research-state condition or a researcher-generated new research direction. |
| **Question resolution** | Achieving sufficient understanding to consider a research question answered or settled. Resolution may occur through interpretation construction, empirical evidence, or further source discovery. |

## 2. Minimum Options

### Option A — Automatic Generation from Research-State Conditions
Every unresolved contradiction, gap, uncertainty, tension, variation, or anomaly automatically becomes a research question.

**Problem:** Creates unsustainable noise; treats all uncertainties as equally worthy of investigation; violates minimum-decision principle; removes researcher judgment; prevents prioritization or focus.

### Option B — Pure Researcher Designation without Grounding
Only the researcher's explicit designation creates a research question, without requiring substantive grounding in the research domain or objectives.

**Problem:** Arbitrary; permits baseless speculation; no accountability to research domain or objectives; undermines methodological rigor.

### Option C — Researcher-Identified Questions with Substantive Grounding
A research question is identified when:
- The researcher judges it worth investigating
- It is substantively grounded in either (a) documented conditions in the current research state, or (b) articulated domain-grounded reasoning introducing a new research direction
- It pertains to the research domain (A2-C1)

**Test against upstream:** Preserves researcher authority (D1) while requiring substantive grounding; does not restrict questions to documented deficiencies; maintains connection to research objectives; respects domain boundaries (A2-C1).

**Verdict:** Option C is the minimum viable and founding-consistent standard.

## 3. Research-Question Identification Procedure

The following procedure governs how the researcher identifies research questions.

**Step 1 — Identify potential origins**

Possible origins include:

**From documented research-state conditions:**
- Unresolved genuine contradictions (A5)
- Acknowledged tensions or variations in the available claim landscape
- Explicit gaps or limitations acknowledged in the current interpretation (A6)
- Uncertain or provisional aspects of the current interpretation
- Empirical anomalies or unexpected results from prior testing
- Downstream departures (A6 Rule 12) revealing interpretive ambiguities or limitations

**From researcher-generated inquiry:**
- New theoretical possibilities grounded in the research domain
- Novel comparisons or extensions within the domain
- Unexplored market phenomena within domain scope
- Questions arising from trading experience, external knowledge, or research objectives
- Deliberate expansion or refinement of research focus within domain boundaries

**Step 2 — Assess substantive grounding**

The potential question must satisfy:

**Domain relevance:** Does it pertain to trading knowledge, market behavior, price mechanics, trading methods, or concepts relevant to trading-model development (A2-C1)?

**Substantive grounding:** Either:
- (a) It addresses something not adequately answered by the current interpretation and available claim landscape, OR
- (b) It introduces a new research direction grounded in articulated domain reasoning and research objectives

A question is not adequately answered if:
- The current interpretation does not address it
- The interpretation explicitly acknowledges uncertainty, gaps, or limitations on the topic
- The interpretation provides a conceptual answer but empirical validation is absent and warranted
- The question investigates, evaluates, or extends the interpretation itself (rather than merely asking what the interpretation states)

**Articulated reasoning:** If the question arises from researcher-generated inquiry (b), the researcher must be able to articulate substantive domain-grounded reasoning for why it is worth investigating, even if that reasoning is not yet documented in source claims.

**Step 3 — Researcher identification**

The researcher formally identifies the question as worth investigating. This is a judgment call constrained by substantive grounding but not mechanically determined.

The researcher may:
- Identify the question and pursue it
- Acknowledge a potential question exists but defer or decline investigation
- Refine, narrow, or broaden a question before pursuing it

**Step 4 — Preserve the question without forcing resolution**

Identifying a research question does not require:
- Immediate answering
- Hypothesis formation
- Test design
- Empirical operationalization
- Interpretation modification

Research questions may remain open indefinitely.

## 4. Binding Methodology Rules

1. **A research question is substantively grounded in the research domain and research objectives.** It may arise from documented conditions in the current research state (contradictions, gaps, limitations, anomalies) or from researcher-generated inquiry that introduces new research directions within the domain, provided the researcher can articulate substantive domain-grounded reasoning.

2. **Not every contradiction, gap, uncertainty, tension, variation, anomaly, or curiosity automatically becomes a research question.** The researcher identifies which are worth investigating based on substantive grounding and judgment.

3. **Research-question identification is distinct from answering the question.** Identifying a question does not require knowing the answer, having a hypothesis, having a test design, or having empirical results. B1 concerns identification; later stages address answering.

4. **Research questions may arise from multiple origins, including researcher-generated new directions.** Valid origins include: documented research-state conditions (unresolved contradictions, tensions, gaps, limitations, uncertainties, empirical anomalies, downstream-departure findings) and researcher-generated grounded inquiry (new theoretical possibilities, novel comparisons, unexplored phenomena, deliberate research-focus expansion).

5. **A research question need not be immediately testable or empirically operationalizable at B1.** Empirical testability is addressed in later stages. Some research questions may be conceptual, definitional, or structural rather than empirical.

6. **Identifying a research question does not modify the current authoritative interpretation (A6).** The interpretation remains authoritative until explicitly revised. The question identifies what requires further investigation without changing what the researcher currently believes.

7. **Identifying a research question does not automatically trigger interpretation reconsideration under A7.** Research-question identification and A7 reconsideration triggers are distinct processes with different conditions. An A7 trigger (e.g., new contradictory claim, changed A5 status) may independently generate research questions, but identifying a research question does not itself create an A7 trigger.

8. **Research questions may remain open, be refined, narrowed, broadened, combined, split, or retired without being answered.** No mandatory resolution timeline or closure requirement exists.

9. **The researcher has authority to identify research questions.** This authority is constrained by substantive grounding in the research domain and research objectives, but within those constraints the researcher exercises judgment about what is worth investigating.

10. **Research questions must pertain to the research domain.** Per A2-C1, the domain is trading knowledge, market behavior, price mechanics, trading methods, or concepts relevant to trading-model development. Questions outside this domain are not Project Minore research questions.

11. **Questions asking what the current interpretation states are not research questions — the interpretation itself answers them.** However, questions investigating, evaluating, empirically testing, comparing, or challenging the current interpretation are valid research questions, provided the researcher has substantive grounds for the inquiry. The existence of an interpretation does not preclude research questions about whether the interpretation is empirically supported, complete, or optimal.

12. **Arbitrary curiosity is distinguished from legitimate inquiry by articulated substantive domain-grounded reasoning.** The discriminator is not whether the question already appears in the available claim landscape, but whether the researcher can articulate substantive domain-grounded reasoning connecting the question to research objectives.

## 6. Ratified Decision

**Option C — Researcher-Identified Questions with Substantive Grounding**

B1 establishes that research questions are:
- Substantively grounded in the research domain and research objectives
- May arise from documented conditions in the current research state (contradictions, gaps, tensions, limitations, anomalies, empirical findings, downstream-departure discoveries) OR from researcher-generated grounded inquiry introducing new research directions
- Pertinent to the research domain (A2-C1 boundary)
- Identified by the researcher as worth investigating (researcher authority with substantive constraint)
- Distinct from answering, hypothesis formation, testing, or interpretation modification

Not every contradiction, gap, uncertainty, or curiosity becomes a research question. The researcher identifies which are worth pursuing based on substantive grounding and judgment.

The discriminator for arbitrary curiosity vs. legitimate inquiry is the researcher's ability to articulate substantive domain-grounded reasoning, not prior existence in the claim landscape.
