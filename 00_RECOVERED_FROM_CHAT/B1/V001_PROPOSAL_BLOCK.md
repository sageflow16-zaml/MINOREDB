## 1. Exact Semantic Distinctions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Research question** | A substantively grounded question about the research domain that the researcher identifies as worth investigating, arising from gaps, uncertainties, contradictions, limitations, or well-founded inquiry in the current research state. |
| **Source claim** | What a source said (A1–A2). Immutable and attributable (D1). Research questions may arise from claims but are not themselves claims. |
| **Researcher interpretation** | The current authoritative understanding of a concept (A6). Research questions may identify what the interpretation does not adequately address, but identifying a question does not modify the interpretation. |
| **Unresolved contradiction** | Claims that cannot both be true simultaneously (A5), preserved without forced reconciliation. May be an origin for research questions but does not automatically become one. |
| **Research gap** | An aspect of the research domain where the current interpretation or available claim landscape provides insufficient, uncertain, incomplete, or absent understanding. |
| **Hypothesis** | A testable proposition formulated in response to a research question (later stages; not B1). Research questions precede hypotheses. |
| **Mere curiosity** | A wondering or speculative question that lacks substantive grounding in the research domain or current research state. |
| **Research-question origin** | The condition in the current research state (contradiction, gap, tension, limitation, anomaly, or grounded inquiry) that gives rise to identifying a research question. |
| **Question resolution** | Achieving sufficient understanding to consider a research question answered or settled. Resolution may occur through interpretation construction, empirical evidence, or further source discovery. |

## 2. Minimum Options

### Option A — Automatic Generation from Research-State Conditions
Every unresolved contradiction, gap, uncertainty, tension, variation, or anomaly automatically becomes a research question.

**Problem:** Creates unsustainable noise; treats all uncertainties as equally worthy of investigation; violates minimum-decision principle; removes researcher judgment; prevents prioritization or focus.

### Option B — Pure Researcher Designation
Only the researcher's explicit designation creates a research question, without requiring grounding in the research state.

**Problem:** Arbitrary; permits baseless speculation; no accountability to research domain or available evidence; disconnects questions from research state; undermines methodological rigor.

### Option C — Researcher-Identified Questions Grounded in Substantive Research-State Conditions
A research question is identified when:
- A substantive gap, uncertainty, contradiction, limitation, anomaly, or well-founded inquiry exists in the current research state
- The researcher judges it worth investigating
- It pertains to the research domain (A2-C1)

**Test against upstream:** Preserves researcher authority (D1) while requiring substantive grounding; maintains connection to research state (A5, A6); respects domain boundaries (A2-C1); does not force automatic question generation.

**Verdict:** Option C is the minimum viable and founding-consistent standard.

## 3. Research-Question Identification Procedure

The following procedure governs how the researcher identifies research questions from the current research state.

**Step 1 — Identify potential origins in the current research state**

Possible origins include:
- Unresolved genuine contradictions (A5)
- Acknowledged tensions or variations in the available claim landscape
- Explicit gaps or limitations acknowledged in the current interpretation (A6)
- Uncertain or provisional aspects of the current interpretation
- Empirical anomalies or unexpected results from prior testing
- Downstream departures (A6 Rule 12) that reveal interpretive ambiguities or limitations
- Researcher-generated inquiry grounded in the research domain and current research state

Not all of these automatically become research questions. They are potential origins.

**Step 2 — Assess substantive grounding**

The potential question must satisfy:
- **Domain relevance:** Does it pertain to trading knowledge, market behavior, price mechanics, trading methods, or concepts relevant to trading-model development (A2-C1)?
- **Research-state connection:** Does it address something not adequately answered by the current interpretation and available claim landscape?
- **Substantive basis:** Is it grounded in actual contradictions, gaps, tensions, limitations, or evidence rather than pure speculation?

**Step 3 — Researcher identification**

The researcher formally identifies the question as worth investigating. This is a judgment call constrained by substantive grounding but not mechanically determined.

The researcher may:
- Identify the question and pursue it
- Acknowledge a potential question exists but defer or decline investigation
- Refine, narrow, or broaden a question before pursuing it

**Step 4 — Preserve the question without forcing resolution**

Identifying a research question does not require:
- Immediate answering
- Hypothesis formation
- Test design
- Empirical operationalization
- Interpretation modification

Research questions may remain open indefinitely.

## 4. Binding Methodology Rules

1. **A research question is substantively grounded in the current research state.** Not every contradiction, gap, uncertainty, tension, variation, anomaly, or curiosity automatically becomes a research question. The researcher identifies which are worth investigating based on substantive grounding and judgment.

2. **Research-question identification is distinct from answering the question.** Identifying a question does not require knowing the answer, having a hypothesis, having a test design, or having empirical results. B1 concerns identification; later stages address answering.

3. **Research questions may arise from multiple origins:** Unresolved contradictions (A5), acknowledged tensions or variations, gaps or limitations in the current interpretation (A6), uncertain or provisional interpretations, empirical anomalies, downstream departures revealing issues, or researcher-generated inquiry grounded in the research domain and current research state.

4. **A research question need not be immediately testable or empirically operationalizable at B1.** Empirical testability is addressed in later stages. Some research questions may be conceptual, definitional, or structural rather than empirical.

5. **Identifying a research question does not modify the current authoritative interpretation (A6).** The interpretation remains authoritative until explicitly revised. The question identifies what requires further investigation without changing what the researcher currently believes.

6. **Identifying a research question does not automatically trigger interpretation reconsideration under A7.** Research-question identification and A7 reconsideration triggers are distinct processes with different conditions. A research question may exist without requiring interpretation reconsideration, and a reconsideration trigger may exist without generating a research question.

7. **Research questions may remain open, be refined, narrowed, broadened, combined, split, or retired without being answered.** No mandatory resolution timeline or closure requirement exists.

8. **The researcher has authority to identify research questions.** This authority is constrained by substantive grounding in the research state and domain relevance, but within those constraints the researcher exercises judgment about what is worth investigating.

9. **Research questions must pertain to the research domain.** Per A2-C1, the domain is trading knowledge, market behavior, price mechanics, trading methods, or concepts relevant to trading-model development. Questions outside this domain are not Project Minore research questions.

10. **A question already adequately answered by the current interpretation and available claim landscape is not a valid research question.** If the interpretation already provides a settled answer without acknowledged gaps or limitations, identifying it as an open research question contradicts the interpretation's authoritative status.

## 5. Adversarial Tests

Current state: Two claims about Optimal Trade Entry level (50% vs. 61.8%) classified by A5 as Genuine Contradiction (unresolved). Current interpretation acknowledges the contradiction but provisionally adopts a position.

Potential research question: *"What is the empirically optimal retracement level for Optimal Trade Entry?"*

- Domain-relevant: Yes (trading method)
- Grounding: Genuine unresolved contradiction in foundational claims
- Substantive basis: Yes
- Researcher judgment: If the researcher identifies this as worth investigating → Valid research question

**Framework handling:** ✓ Unresolved contradiction provides substantive origin; researcher authority determines whether to pursue

**Test 2 — Mere curiosity with no grounding**

Speculative wondering: *"Do Fair Value Gaps work better on Tuesdays than Wednesdays?"*

- Domain-relevant: Yes (market behavior)
- Grounding in current research state: No claim, interpretation, contradiction, or evidence addresses day-of-week effects
- Substantive basis: Pure speculation without grounding

**Classification:** Mere curiosity, not a valid research question at this stage. Could become valid if future claims or evidence introduce day-of-week considerations into the research state.

**Framework handling:** ✓ Lacks substantive grounding; correctly excluded

## 6. Recommended Decision

**Option C — Researcher-Identified Questions Grounded in Substantive Research-State Conditions**

B1 establishes that research questions are:
- Substantively grounded in the current research state (contradictions, gaps, tensions, limitations, anomalies, or well-founded inquiry)
- Pertinent to the research domain (A2-C1 boundary)
- Identified by the researcher as worth investigating (researcher authority with substantive constraint)
- Distinct from answering, hypothesis formation, testing, or interpretation modification

Not every contradiction, gap, uncertainty, or curiosity becomes a research question. The researcher identifies which are worth pursuing based on substantive grounding and judgment.
