SEMANTIC CONSISTENCY AUDIT — DECISION B1
Upstream Authority: All ratified decisions through A7 inclusive, plus A7-C1 and A7-C2.

Point 1 Finding — Research Questions Must Arise from Current Research State
Exact tension:

B1 Step 2 requires: "Research-state connection: Does it address something not adequately answered by the current interpretation and available claim landscape?"

B1 Rule 1 requires: "A research question is substantively grounded in the current research state."

The task constraints explicitly stated: "Do not assume that a research question must originate only from a defect in the current interpretation."

B1 appears to require every research question to be grounded in an existing documented gap, contradiction, limitation, or deficiency in the current research state.

Upstream authority test:

The Founding Definition's Core Problem Q6 asks: "What does market data actually support or contradict?" This implies research questions about market behavior that may not yet be addressed by any existing interpretation or claim landscape.

Core Capability 6: "Identifying unanswered research questions." This does not restrict identification to gaps in the current interpretation — it may include genuinely new questions.

Necessity test:

Can a researcher legitimately ask: "Do liquidity patterns differ meaningfully across different market regimes?" even if:

No current interpretation addresses market regimes
No source claim mentions regimes
No contradiction, gap, or defect exists in the current research state regarding regimes
This could be:

A theoretically motivated inquiry
A novel research direction
An extension of the research domain
Grounded in the researcher's trading experience or external knowledge
Is this arbitrary speculation, or legitimate research-domain inquiry?

The distinction B1 needs is:

Arbitrary curiosity: baseless speculation with no substantive connection to the research domain or research objectives
Research-state-responsive inquiry: questions arising from documented gaps, contradictions, limitations in the current research state
Researcher-generated grounded inquiry: questions introducing new research directions, grounded in the research domain and research objectives, even without prior documentation in the current research state
B1 currently conflates the second and third categories under "grounded in current research state."

Whether a real inconsistency exists:

Yes. B1's current formulation makes "current research-state deficiency" a necessary condition for valid research questions. This is overly restrictive and contradicts the task constraint. It prevents the researcher from introducing genuinely new research directions.

Minimum correction required:

Revise Step 2 and Rule 1:

"Research-state connection: Does it address something not adequately answered by the current interpretation and available claim landscape, OR does it introduce a genuinely new research direction grounded in the research domain and research objectives?"

"A research question is substantively grounded in the research domain and research objectives. It may arise from documented conditions in the current research state (contradictions, gaps, limitations) or from researcher-generated inquiry that introduces new research directions within the domain."

Add a clarifying rule distinguishing arbitrary speculation from legitimate new-direction inquiry based on domain relevance and explicit researcher reasoning, not prior documentation.

Point 2 Finding — "Mere Curiosity" Classification (Test 2)
Exact tension:

Test 2 rejects: "Do Fair Value Gaps work better on Tuesdays than Wednesdays?"

Reasoning: "No claim, interpretation, contradiction, or evidence addresses day-of-week effects" → "Pure speculation without grounding" → Mere curiosity.

But consider: the researcher might have noticed anecdotal patterns, read external research on calendar effects in markets, or have a theoretical reason to explore temporal variations. The question is:

Domain-relevant (market behavior)
Conceptually coherent
Empirically investigable
The rejection is based solely on absence from the current claim landscape.

Upstream authority test:

A2-C1 defines domain relevance: trading knowledge, market behavior, price mechanics, trading methods. Calendar effects on market behavior are within this domain.

The Founding Definition does not require every research question to have prior claim support.

Necessity test:

The correct distinction is:

Arbitrary curiosity: No articulated reason, domain connection, or research objective. Example: "Do FVGs work better when I'm wearing a blue shirt?"
Legitimate exploratory inquiry: Domain-relevant, conceptually coherent, researcher has an articulated reason (even if that reason is not yet documented in source claims). Example: "Do calendar effects influence FVG behavior?" if the researcher has external knowledge of calendar effects in markets or observes patterns worth investigating.
B1 currently uses "grounding in current research state" as the sole discriminator. This collapses legitimate new-direction inquiry into "mere curiosity."

Whether a real inconsistency exists:

Yes. Test 2's rejection conflates "absence from current claim landscape" with "baseless speculation." The correct test is whether the researcher has substantive domain-grounded reasoning for the question, not whether it already appears in the claim landscape.

Minimum correction required:

Revise Test 2 to distinguish:

"Speculative question with articulated domain-grounded reasoning: 'Do Fair Value Gaps work better on Tuesdays than Wednesdays?' If the researcher has no articulated reason beyond idle curiosity → Mere curiosity (invalid). If the researcher has external knowledge of calendar effects or observed patterns worth investigating → Legitimate exploratory inquiry (valid if researcher identifies it as worth pursuing)."

The discriminator is researcher's ability to articulate substantive domain-grounded reasoning, not prior existence in claim landscape.

Point 3 Finding — Rule 10 and Questions About "Answered" Interpretations
Exact tension:

Rule 10: "A question already adequately answered by the current interpretation and available claim landscape is not a valid research question."

Test 8 applies this: "What is a Fair Value Gap?" is rejected because the current interpretation provides a complete answer.

But consider:

A6 Rule 12: Downstream research may explicitly depart from the current interpretation with documented reasoning
A6 Rule 9: Interpretation is not empirical validation
The current interpretation may be wrong, incomplete, or require empirical testing
A researcher might legitimately ask: "What is a Fair Value Gap empirically?" even though the interpretation already defines it conceptually. The question is not "what does the interpretation say?" but "what does market data show?"

Upstream authority test:

A6 establishes the current interpretation as authoritative but not infallible or empirically validated. Downstream research may question it.

The Founding Definition Core Problem Q6: "What does market data actually support or contradict?" — this implies questions about whether interpretations are empirically valid.

Necessity test:

Rule 10's intent is correct: don't re-ask questions the interpretation has already settled. But the formulation is too broad. It prevents empirical challenges to the interpretation.

The correct boundary:

Already settled conceptually: "What does our current interpretation define FVG as?" → Not a research question (just refer to the interpretation)
Empirical validation of interpretation: "Does market data support our FVG definition?" → Legitimate research question even though interpretation exists
Alternative conceptual framework: "Should FVG be defined differently?" → Legitimate if researcher has substantive reasoning, even though interpretation exists
Whether a real inconsistency exists:

Yes. Rule 10 conflates "interpretation provides an answer" with "question is settled and cannot be investigated." This contradicts the principle that interpretations are authoritative references but not empirically validated truth (A6 Rule 9).

Minimum correction required:

Revise Rule 10:

"A question about what the current interpretation states is not a research question — the interpretation itself answers it. However, questions about whether the interpretation is empirically supported, complete, or optimal may be valid research questions even when an interpretation exists, provided the researcher has substantive grounds for the inquiry."

Add clarification that conceptual questions settled by the current authoritative interpretation are distinct from empirical or evaluative questions about that interpretation.

Point 4 Finding — B1 vs. A7 Relationship (Rule 6)
Exact tension:

This is stated but not elaborated. Test whether the relationship is correctly bounded.

Research question identified: "Under what conditions do Fair Value Gaps fill?"

Materially affect the documented interpretation foundation? Possibly, if the interpretation explicitly acknowledged the question or if the question reveals a gap the interpretation should have addressed.
Could identifying a research question itself be an A7 Permissive trigger (relevant, substantive basis, non-foundational)?

Upstream authority test:

A7 triggers are based on changes to upstream outputs (claim landscape, A5 status, etc.). B1 is not an upstream output to A6/A7 — it's a parallel or downstream process.

However, B1's identification of a gap might reflect a researcher's evolving understanding that could feed back into A7 as "researcher re-evaluation without new upstream change" (A7 Rule 9 — Permissive).

Necessity test:

Does B1 need to address the A7 relationship more carefully?

The current Rule 6 correctly states they are distinct. The risk is that someone might think: "I identified a research question about an interpretation gap, therefore A7 requires reconsideration."

The correct relationship:

Identifying a research question acknowledges something worth investigating
A7 reconsideration is triggered by changes materially affecting the interpretation foundation
These are orthogonal unless the act of identifying the question itself constitutes recognizing a material change
Whether a real inconsistency exists:

No fundamental inconsistency, but Rule 6 could be clearer about the directional relationship: identifying a question does not trigger A7, but an A7 trigger (e.g., new contradictory claim) might generate a research question.

Minimum correction required:

Narrow clarification to Rule 6:

"Identifying a research question does not automatically trigger interpretation reconsideration under A7. Research questions and A7 reconsideration triggers are distinct: A7 governs when the current interpretation must or may be reconsidered based on changes to upstream outputs; B1 governs when the researcher identifies something worth investigating. An A7 trigger may generate research questions, but identifying a research question does not itself create an A7 trigger."

Conceptually defined but not empirically tested?

Upstream authority test:

A6 permits provisional interpretations that explicitly acknowledge uncertainty, limitations, or pending validation. A provisional answer is still an authoritative interpretation.

Necessity test:

If the interpretation provisionally defines something with acknowledged uncertainty, is a question about that topic "adequately answered"?

Example: Interpretation says "Order Blocks are defined as consolidations before impulses, provisionally pending empirical clarification of formation conditions."

Question: "What are the empirical formation conditions for Order Blocks?"

Partial. "Adequately answered" is under-defined. The standard should reference whether the interpretation explicitly acknowledges gaps, limitations, or provisional status.

Minimum correction required:

"A question is not adequately answered if: (1) the current interpretation does not address it, (2) the interpretation explicitly acknowledges uncertainty, gaps, or limitations on the topic, or (3) the interpretation provides a conceptual answer but empirical validation is absent and warranted."
